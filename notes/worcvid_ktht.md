## What this batch is
Fourteen places in downtown Worcester and on Salisbury Street, from a "videos" session (job 9) working one Old World Exploration video about Worcester (the record is `source/videos/20240809_cnGGQsNrjhQ.md`; 42 names: 14 added, 5 already on the Atlas, 6 gone, 2 not found, 14 not checked, 1 elsewhere). Added: Antiquarian Hall (1910), the Salisbury Street National Guard armory (1889 to 1890, now Veterans, Inc.), the Worcester Art Museum, the Cathedral of Saint Paul, City Hall (1898), the Union and former Central Congregational churches, the Donohue federal building and courthouse (1930 to 1931), Mechanics Hall (1857), Pilgrim Congregational Church, the Slater Building (1907), Union Station (1911), Fenwick and O'Kane halls at Holy Cross, and Boynton Hall at WPI. Facts are from Wikipedia and the Worcester Register lists (read with `wiki_raw.py`), Lost New England and Historic Buildings of Massachusetts (armory, Central Church, Boynton Hall), WPI's historic-district exhibit, and the hours pages of the American Antiquarian Society, Mechanics Hall, the Worcester Art Museum and the City of Worcester. The cathedral's own site (cathedralofsaintpaul.net) answered 403, so it has no Mass times. Not checked: anything on the ground, interiors, present use of the Union and Pilgrim churches, Union Station's opening hours, campus visitor rules at Holy Cross and WPI, and most parking. The cathedral's dates disagree within Wikipedia's article (completed 1874, or built 1868 to 1889), and Central Church's year is given as 1885 or 1886; both are `conf='mixed'`.
