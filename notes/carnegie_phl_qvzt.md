## What this batch is

Twenty-three of Philadelphia's 25 Carnegie branch libraries, part of the pass the owner asked for to put the buildings Andrew Carnegie and the Rockefellers paid for on the map. Philadelphia had one grant of $1,500,000 (January 1903) and built its branches from 1905 to 1930; Wyoming, dedicated in October 1930, is called the last Carnegie library built anywhere. None of them was on the Atlas before.

Added: sixteen working branches (Chestnut Hill, Cobbs Creek, Falls of Schuylkill, Haddington, Kingsessing, Lillian Marrero (Lehigh Avenue), Logan, McPherson Square, Oak Lane, Paschalville, Thomas F. Donatucci Sr. (Passyunk), Richmond, Tacony, Holmesburg (Thomas Holme), Walnut Street West and Wyoming); four old branch buildings now put to other uses (Germantown, now Center in the Park; Manayunk, now apartments; the old South Philadelphia branch on Broad Street; Southwark); and the sites of three that are gone (Frankford, replaced in 1959 on the same spot; Spring Garden, torn down by the Community College in 1974 or 1975; Greenwich, at 4th and Shunk Streets).

Sources: the Philadelphia Historical Commission's 2020 nomination of the Carnegie Libraries Thematic Historic District (architects, builders, styles, walls, listing dates), Ken Finkel's census of the 25 branches on the PhillyHistory blog (2019), the Encyclopedia of Greater Philadelphia, the Free Library's own branch pages (hours, histories; read in November 2026), city Rebuild pages and press releases, and local papers, read with the web fetch tool. Hours are from the Free Library's own pages only. Pins are ArcGIS geocoder points for the street addresses, or OpenStreetMap points where the geocoder missed the building, all looked at on the leaf-off aerial.

Not checked: the use of the old South Philadelphia branch (only the 2020 nomination says the Philadelphia Performing Arts Charter School); who owns Southwark's building; whether Germantown's walls are schist (the nomination) or brick (a 2011 article); the opening dates where sources differ (Chestnut Hill 1907 or 1909, Logan 1917 to 1919); which corner Greenwich stood on. Falls of Schuylkill was closed for heating work (to reopen in January 2027, the library said) and McPherson Square is closed until spring 2028, so both have hours "?". Red brick is "yes" for Richmond and Southwark (the nomination's text) and for Wyoming, Oak Lane and Paschalville (Commons pictures looked at); "no" for schist, stucco, terra cotta and limestone fronts and for Passyunk, whose picture shows buff brick; Germantown is "no" from its picture, though a 2011 article calls it brick; "?" for the rest, where the nomination says brick without a colour.

Pictures: twenty places have a Commons picture chosen by looking at the finder's sheet (Richmond's is a Historic American Buildings Survey photograph). The three sites of torn-down branches have none.

## Tips

- The city's nomination PDF (phila.gov/media/20200109130220/Historic-District-Carnegie-Libraries.pdf) gives architect, builder, style, wall material and Register date for every surviving branch; it is the best single source.
- The Free Library's current pages are at libwww.freelibrary.org/locations/<branch>; the older branches/branch.cfm pages carry stale hours.
- The geocoder's points for Lehigh Avenue, Wyoming and Holmesburg missed the buildings; OpenStreetMap's library points were right.
