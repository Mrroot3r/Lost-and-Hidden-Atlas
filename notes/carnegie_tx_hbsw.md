## What this batch is

Carnegie libraries of Texas (23 places): 12 standing buildings and 11 sites of torn-down or burned ones, from Wikipedia's state list, the Handbook of Texas, Texas Historical Commission markers, SAH Archipedia, National Register records, HMdb and the operators' own pages. Texas had 34 gifts and 32 public library buildings (Handbook). Hours only from the operators' pages.

## What was not checked

- Pages were read through a page summariser, so wording is close paraphrase.
- Cleburne, Jefferson, Palestine: no hours on an operator page ('?'). Palestine's city library page refused the fetch.
- Terrell: hours were found only on a city tourism page, which also gives a second, conflicting address, so hours are '?'.
- Jefferson: walls (limestone or tawny brick), architect and grant ($7,500 or $10,000) not settled. The Wikipedia article on it appears to mix in Jefferson, Iowa; its picture, if any, should be checked.
- Franklin and Gainesville pins sit beside the building ('site').
- No pictures were opened for the walls; brick comes from the sources' words.

## Leads not finished

- Sulphur Springs (100 Jefferson St W): Wikipedia's list says it stands as Alliance Bank; Archipedia and Texas Escapes say the 1910 Tilton library was demolished. Needs a look.
- Wiley University, Marshall: the Carnegie library is said to be the Willis J. King Administration Building (1907 or 1910), but Clio calls Thirkield Hall (1919) the oldest building on campus. No pin for the building.
- Houston "Colored Carnegie Library" (1913, William Sidney Pittman, Frederick and Robin streets, Fourth Ward): razed 1962 or in the 1970s; the area was replatted (Two Allen Center or a newer Carnegie branch). Site not pinned.
- Sites not found: Brownwood (razed 1965), Clarksville (burned 1980), Dallas branch (likely Oak Cliff, 1914), Greenville (opened 1904, razed 1953), Memphis (razed mid-1980s), Pecos (early 1960s), Vernon (late 1970s). The unt.edu Portal to Texas History has the old newspapers but showed a verification screen.
