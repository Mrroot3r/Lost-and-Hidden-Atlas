## What this batch is
Sixteen standing places in Spokane, Washington, from the names in one video about the city's old buildings (Old World Exploration, October 2022; the record is source/videos/20221013_316A1oucsus.md). The video was used only as a list of names; every fact is from a source opened for this batch. Added: the Masonic Temple, the Elks Temple and the Spokane Club on Riverside Avenue, the Cathedral of Our Lady of Lourdes, the Cathedral of St. John the Evangelist, St. Aloysius Church at Gonzaga, the Spokane County Courthouse, the U.S. Post Office, Courthouse, and Custom House, The Davenport Hotel, the Review, Empire State, Old National Bank (U.S. Bank) and Paulsen Medical and Dental buildings, the Monroe Street Bridge, the Great Northern clock tower in Riverfront Park and the old Holy Names Academy (now apartments). Sources: Wikipedia read as raw text (the Riverside Avenue Historic District article covers the Masonic Temple, Elks Temple, Spokane Club and Lourdes cathedral building by building), the Spokane County Register list, and the National Register record and form for the Holy Names Academy Building. Pins are address points or article coordinates, all looked at on the aerial; the Review and Empire State pins were on the street and were moved onto the buildings. Hours: only the Cathedral of St. John's own page could be read; the Lourdes cathedral (spokanecathedral.com) and St. Aloysius (stalschurch.org) pages refused automated reading, so their Mass times are '?'. Not checked: anything on the ground; whether the Masonic Temple, the Davenport's public rooms, the courthouse or the federal building can be visited and when; Riverfront Park hours; parking owners and fees downtown. Red brick: from a source for the Spokane Club and Holy Names Academy, from a Commons photograph for the Lourdes cathedral; most others stay '?'.
