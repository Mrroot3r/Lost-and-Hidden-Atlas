## What this batch is

Carnegie libraries of Arkansas, Louisiana and Mississippi (22 places): 16 standing buildings and 6 sites. Arkansas 4, Louisiana 8 (five of them New Orleans libraries), Mississippi 10. Sources: Wikipedia's three state lists and articles, the Encyclopedia of Arkansas, SAH Archipedia, National Register nominations, New Orleans City Archives and Public Library, The Historic New Orleans Collection, BlackPast, HMdb and the operators' own pages. Hours only from the operators' pages.

## What was not checked

- Fort Smith: privately owned since 2024 (owner not named here); whether KFSM is still a tenant (2024 and 2025 articles disagree); wall material from a strong source; architect.
- Greenwood: present use not found. West Point: present occupant (Chamber of Commerce or a community development group), date and architect not found.
- Meridian Museum of Art: whether it was the 1883 First Presbyterian Church (Wikipedia) or built 1912 to 1913 (state survey); museum hours not found on its own page.
- Napoleon Avenue branch: closed for renovation May 2025; the library's updated page says it has reopened. National Register listing not confirmed.
- Canal Street branch: whether the yoga studio is still there.
- Greenwood and West Point pins are from interpolated addresses ('area'); Dryades is 'site'.
- No pictures were opened for the walls; brick comes from the sources' words.

## Leads not finished

- Lake Charles (1904, Pujo and Bilbo streets): replaced by the 1952 Carnegie Memorial Branch at 411 Pujo; whether the old building was torn down and whether the new one stands on the same lot is not settled (the library's own pages disagree).
- Jackson, Mississippi ($25,000, 1911): whether it was built, where, and its fate were not found.
- Millsaps College (grants of 1906 and 1923): which building, and whether it stands, not found.
- University of Mississippi, Bryant Hall (1911): stands, but its Carnegie link rests only on Wikipedia's list; no pin found for the building.
- Mississippi Industrial College, Holly Springs: a Carnegie Auditorium (1923), not a library; left out.
