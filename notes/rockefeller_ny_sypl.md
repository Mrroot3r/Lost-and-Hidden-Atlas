## What this batch is

Ten buildings in New York that John D. Rockefeller or his son John D. Rockefeller Jr. paid for, the Rockefeller half of the Carnegie and Rockefeller pass the owner asked for. In Manhattan: Riverside Church, Central Presbyterian Church (built as Park Avenue Baptist Church), the Cloisters, International House, Founder's Hall at Rockefeller University, the original Rockefeller Center, the Rockefeller Apartments and the Dunbar Apartments. In the Bronx: the Thomas Garden Apartments. In Poughkeepsie: Vassar College's Rockefeller Hall. Philipsburg Manor, restored with Rockefeller Jr.'s money, was already on the map (`data_midh_luaq.py`).

Sources: Wikipedia, read as raw text; the National Park Service; the Vassar Encyclopedia; Britannica; and 6 to Celebrate. The pins are ArcGIS geocoder points for the street addresses or OpenStreetMap outlines, each checked on the aerial. Rockefeller Center, the Dunbar Apartments and Founder's Hall are at site precision.

Not checked:
- Opening hours for Riverside Church, Central Presbyterian and the Cloisters. No page read gave them, so each is `hours='?'`.
- Whether the public can go into Founder's Hall.
- The date Rockefeller Center was made a National Historic Landmark.
- Whether Rockefeller Sr. gave money for Central Presbyterian as well as his son. One section of its Wikipedia page says both did.

Red brick is "yes" for Vassar's Rockefeller Hall (from its Commons picture) and for the Thomas Garden Apartments (from 6 to Celebrate). It is "no" where a source or picture shows stone or limestone, and "?" for International House and the Dunbar Apartments.

Pictures: pictures from Wikimedia Commons were chosen for International House, Rockefeller Center and Vassar's Rockefeller Hall. None fitted the Thomas Garden Apartments. The other six take their picture from their own Wikipedia article.

## Tips

- A building "named for" a Rockefeller is not always one the family paid for. Find the gift in a source before adding it.
- Rockefeller Jr. gave housing as well as institutions. The Dunbar and Thomas Garden apartments were cooperatives he financed in 1926 to 1928.
