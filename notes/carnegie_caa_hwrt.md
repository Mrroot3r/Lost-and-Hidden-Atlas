## What this batch is
Part 1 of California's Carnegie libraries: the rows of Wikipedia's list for towns A to G, and Pomona College's library in Claremont (the state's other college Carnegie, Mills College in Oakland, is left to part 2). 32 places: 23 standing and 9 sites of libraries pulled down (Azusa, Berkeley, Coalinga, Corona, El Centro, Escondido, Fresno, Fullerton, Glendale). None was on the Atlas before. The backbone is the state's National Register cover document, California Carnegie Libraries (1990), read whole, with Wikipedia's articles, the Dixon nomination, three Pacific Coast Architecture Database pages, and the users' own pages for hours.

## What was not checked
- Pins were looked at on the aerial: the standing buildings sit on buildings (Ferndale, in haze, is marked as near the building). Dixon and Exeter are interpolated street addresses (area). Pomona College's building is OpenStreetMap's "Carnegie Hall" on College Avenue; no source gave its street address. The corner pins for gone libraries (Berkeley, Corona, Escondido, Fresno, Fullerton, Glendale) mark the crossing only.
- Present use rests on Wikipedia's list where the 1990 survey is older: Antioch (a church now), Auburn (arts centre), Alturas (law offices), Gridley (private office), Alameda (empty since 1998). None was checked on the ground or in recent news.
- Hours not found on an operator's page: Bayliss, Biggs, Dixon (its page showed only holiday hours). Auburn, Antioch and Exeter are left as look from the street.
- Red brick: only Grass Valley (yes), Eureka (some, with granite) and the buff-brick Alameda are settled from sources; the rest are '?' except concrete or stucco buildings.

## Leads not finished
- Chula Vista (1916 grant, vacated 1955, pulled down 1960), Concord (1915 grant, pulled down 1959), Covina (1905, pulled down 1962), Dinuba (built 1916 on land the city gave, pulled down 1975; the county library's page does not say whether the present branch at 150 South I Street is on the site) and East San Diego (1915 grant, pulled down 1964): no source opened said where they stood.
- Fresno: the library stood at 1330 Broadway at Merced Street; the corner was not placed.

## Tips for the next session
- The cover document (Register reference 64500054) is the best single source for all of California: read it with the Register form reader and search it by town. It covers parts 2 and 3 too, and dates from 1990, so check present use elsewhere.
- In this environment the page reader is refused by most hosts other than Wikipedia and the Park Service; the web fetch tool opened museum and library pages.
- The Pacific Coast Architecture Database has pages for many demolished California Carnegie libraries with street addresses (Pomona, Santa Ana, Salinas, San Bernardino, Redding showed up in searches).
- Pictures: Commons is slow without a login, so picture choices for this batch are left for a later pictures pass.
