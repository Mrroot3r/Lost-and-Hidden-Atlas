## What this batch is
Part 3 of California's Carnegie libraries: the rows of Wikipedia's list for towns P to Z (part 1 took A to G and Pomona College, part 2 H to O and Mills College). 46 places: 36 standing buildings and 10 sites of libraries pulled down (Pomona, San Bernardino, San Diego, San Jose's main library, Santa Ana, Santa Cruz's main library, Santa Maria, Santa Rosa, Watts in Los Angeles, Whittier). Sacramento's Carnegie library was already on the Atlas (sacramento-city-library-carnegie) and is not repeated. San Francisco has its old main library (now the Asian Art Museum) and all seven Carnegie branches. The backbone is the state's National Register cover document, California Carnegie Libraries (1990), searched town by town, with Wikipedia's list and articles, three Pacific Coast Architecture Database pages, the Los Angeles Public Library's Carnegie page, the Sonoma County Library's Santa Rosa history, and four museums' own pages for hours.

## What was not checked
- Pins were looked at on the aerial. All are geocoder points, checked against Wikipedia's points only where an article gave one (Paso Robles, Petaluma, Mission branch, East San Jose, Seabright, South San Francisco, Upland). South Pasadena and Willits are interpolated street addresses ('area'). San Diego (E Street between 8th and 9th, which half of the block not found), Santa Ana, Santa Rosa and Whittier are street-corner pins ('area'). Watts rests only on the address in Wikipedia's list.
- Hours came from operators' own pages only for the Asian Art Museum, the Richmond museum, the History Center in San Luis Obispo and the Santa Cruz Museum of Natural History; every other open place is hours='?' (all the libraries, the San Francisco branches, Petaluma, Paso Robles, Riverbank, Roseville, Willows, Turlock, Sonoma's visitor centre). The hours job can fill them.
- Present use rests on Wikipedia's list or the 1990 survey: Patterson (privately owned offices in 1990), Vacaville (privately owned and empty in 1990), Ukiah (privately owned in 1990), Yreka (police station in the survey and Wikipedia's article, empty in the list), Turlock (gutted by fire, being restored inside a new addition, per the list), St. Helena, Willits, Upland. None was checked on the ground or in recent news.
- South Pasadena: the 1990 survey says Carnegie paid only for the 1916 addition and the old building is wrapped inside the 1930 and 1981 rebuilding; Wikipedia's list gives grants in 1906 and 1916. Both are in the entry.
- Red brick: only San Luis Obispo is 'yes' (red brick, sandstone and granite). Petaluma is stone, Yolo and Riverbank wood ('no'). Paso Robles, Richmond, Roseville and Upland have brick pilasters or are "mostly of brick" in the survey, and the Presidio branch is called brick, but no source gave a colour, so they are '?'.
- Paso Robles, Roseville, Vacaville and Willows have temple fronts with columns; no source said whether mutules or triglyphs are there.

## Leads not finished
- Riverside (1901 grant, Burnham and Bliesner's Mission Revival library, opened July 31, 1903, pulled down late 1964): the city library's history puts it at the north-east corner of Seventh and Orange Streets, and the Los Angeles Public Library's page says Riverside's Chinese Memorial Pavilion now stands on the site. Neither the pavilion nor the corner by that name could be placed.
- Redding (1903 grant, built 1902 to 1903, pulled down 1962 for a parking lot): the architecture database says the lot became Library Park in 1990; no address for the park was found in a source.
- Salinas (1907 grant, Jacob Lenzen and Son, opened September 5, 1909, razed 1961): no address in the architecture database.
- San Pedro (1905 grant, Classical Revival, a library from 1906 to 1923, then the Chamber of Commerce and the Seamen's Library, pulled down 1966), Santa Monica's main library (Marsh and Russell, pulled down 1974), Palo Alto (1903 grant, pulled down 1967 for a new city hall), Sebastopol (1911 grant, pulled down 1976, another library built on the site), Santa Cruz Eastside branch (Weeks's twin of Yolo, pulled down 1968), Visalia (Benjamin McDougall, pulled down 1936, replaced by a WPA building), San Leandro (Weeks, pulled down 1959), Watsonville (Weeks's 1903 triumphal arch, pulled down 1975), Porterville, Redwood City, San Mateo, Sanger, Selma, Tulare, Vallejo, Walnut Creek: no source opened said where they stood.
- Watts: Wikipedia's list gives 9901 Grandee Avenue and says a newer library on a different site replaced it in 1960; the Los Angeles Public Library says the Carnegie building was razed in the 1970s. A local history would confirm the address.

## Tips for the next session
- The cover document (Register reference 64500054) is long and its text is one block: pull passages out round each town name with a short script. Section F's style groups (about characters 88,000 to 119,000 of the cleaned text), the "current uses" summary (about 119,000 to 126,000) and the architects section (about 127,000 to 137,000) hold nearly everything per building.
- The page reader could not reach the Pacific Coast Architecture Database here; the web fetch tool opened it. A web search for "pcad.lib.washington.edu <town> Carnegie" finds the page numbers.
- Wikipedia's list prints coordinates only up to Paso Robles in the page reader's text; later rows need a geocoder. The category page "Carnegie libraries in California" lists every article title in one fetch.
- The Los Angeles Public Library's Carnegie page also covers Long Beach, Glendale, Monrovia, San Bernardino and Riverside.
- Pictures: Commons is slow without a login, so picture choices for this batch are left for a later pictures pass.
