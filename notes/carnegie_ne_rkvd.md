## What this batch is

Forty-six Carnegie buildings in Maine (20), New Hampshire (10), Vermont (5) and Connecticut (11), part of the pass the owner asked for to put the buildings Andrew Carnegie and the Rockefellers paid for on the map. They are the town and branch libraries on Wikipedia's state lists and the college buildings Carnegie paid for (University of Maine's Carnegie Hall, Good Will-Hinckley's Carnegie Library, Norwich University's Chaplin Hall, University of New Hampshire's Hamilton Smith Hall). Massachusetts is in its own batch (`carnegie_ma_wqlx`). Rhode Island has no Wikipedia list of Carnegie libraries and was not done.

All forty-six stand. Thirty-six are still libraries (Presque Isle's only in part, at the back of the 1960s building); the rest are a shop building (Freeport), a museum (Unionville), a church (New Haven's Dixwell branch), a radio station's building (New Haven's Davenport branch), college classrooms (Orono, Durham, Northfield), a school library (Good Will-Hinckley), and Bridgeport's two branches, whose use now was not found.

Sources: Wikipedia's state lists and articles, New England Carnegies (necarnegies.com; its notes on present use are mostly about 2005), Buildings of New England, SAH Archipedia, National Register nominations on NPGallery, the libraries', towns' and colleges' own pages, and local papers, read with the web fetch tool. Hours are from the operators' own pages only. Pins are ArcGIS geocoder points for the street addresses, or OpenStreetMap points, all looked at on the state aerials.

Not checked:
- Bridgeport's East and North branches: present use (New England Carnegies, about 2005 and 2009, is the newest source).
- Freeport's old Bartol library: tenant since January 2024.
- Gardiner: what the 1897 Carnegie grant paid for; the building is of 1881 and the library's own history does not mention Carnegie.
- Milo: the grant year (1908 or 1909 in the lists; 1921 in the National Register form and Wikipedia's article, which the place follows).
- Chaplin Hall (Norwich) and Good Will-Hinckley's Carnegie Library: no map read names the building, so both pins are the campus address ("area").
- Hours "?" for Guilford, Milo and Rockland (no page read gives them), Houlton (only an old, undated library page) and Franklin, New Hampshire (the city's site would not load).
- Addresses differ between sources for Milo, Pittsfield, Rumford, Vinalhaven, Raymond and Claremont; the places give both. Wikipedia's Connecticut list gives the wrong address for Bridgeport's North branch (3455 Madison Avenue is the 1995 branch; the Carnegie building is at 2181 Main Street).
- Red brick: "yes" from a source's words (Freeport, Rumford, Berlin) or a Commons picture looked at (Auburn, Gardiner, Guilford, Madison, Milo, Oakland, Good Will-Hinckley, Littleton, Whitefield, Burlington's Fletcher Free Library, New Haven's Davenport branch, Norwalk); "no" for granite, stone, white or buff brick, stucco and wood (Raymond's Dudley-Tucker Library, from its picture); "?" where nothing settled it (Old Town's tinted postcard was not trusted).
- Pictures: 26 places have a Wikimedia Commons photograph chosen from the finder's sheets. None was found that plainly shows the building for Caribou, Fort Fairfield, Houlton, Presque Isle, Waterville, the University of Maine's Carnegie Hall, Berlin, Dover, Rochester, the University of New Hampshire's Hamilton Smith Hall, Morristown, Norwich's Chaplin Hall, Bridgeport's two branches, Derby Neck, New Haven's Fair Haven and Dixwell branches, South Norwalk, Unionville and West Haven.
