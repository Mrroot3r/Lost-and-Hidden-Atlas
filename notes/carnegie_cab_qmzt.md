## What this batch is
Part 2 of California's Carnegie libraries: the rows of Wikipedia's list for towns H to O, and the Margaret Carnegie Library at Mills College in Oakland (Pomona College's was in part 1). 31 places: 25 standing and 6 sites of libraries pulled down (Arroyo Seco in Los Angeles, Huntington Beach, Inglewood, Long Beach, Orange, and Oakland's 23rd Avenue branch on Miller Avenue, burned and pulled down in 2018). None was on the Atlas before. The backbone is the state's National Register cover document, California Carnegie Libraries (1990), searched town by town, with Wikipedia's list and articles, the Los Angeles Public Library's page on its Carnegie branches, Oakland LocalWiki for Miller Avenue, and the users' own pages for hours.

## What was not checked
- Pins were looked at on the aerial; Orosi, by the street, is marked as near the building. Nevada City's geocoded address is about 230 m from Wikipedia's point and is marked 'site'; one of the two is wrong. Mills College is OpenStreetMap's campus point ('area'): no source placed the building on the Oval. Huntington Beach and Long Beach are street-corner pins ('area'). Newman, Oakdale, Orland and the three Oakland branches had no second point to check against; Golden Gate's address geocodes just over the Emeryville line.
- Present use rests on Wikipedia's list where the 1990 survey is older: Lakeport (UC Davis research offices), Lodi (city council chambers), Mill Valley (private home), Monterey (institute offices), Oakdale (private offices), Orland (community centre), Mills College (offices and a reading room). Oxnard's art museum closed in 2019; what the building is used for now was not checked.
- Hours not found on an operator's page: Hanford, Healdsburg (its page showed only a closure for a new exhibition), Lincoln, Lompoc, the three Los Angeles branches (Cahuenga, Lincoln Heights, Vermont Square), Newman, the African American Museum and Library at Oakland, Orosi and Oroville. Hollister's city hall is left as look from the street.
- Red brick: only Newman (red, from the list's words) and the gone Huntington Beach library (red with white trim) are 'yes'. Livermore is buff, Vermont Square cream glazed brick, Cahuenga tawny brick, Healdsburg and Hollister concrete, Orosi wood, Oakdale and Mills stucco. Nevada City is '?': the 1990 survey says concrete blocks, Wikipedia a dark brick front painted white. Lincoln, Lodi and Temescal are brick of a colour no source gave.
- Oxnard's porches stand on Doric columns; no source said whether mutules or triglyphs are there.

## Leads not finished
- Hayward (1906 grant, a Spanish Revival library by Stone and Smith, given $1,750 for 1906 earthquake repairs, pulled down 1949), Hemet (1910 grant, S. L. Pillar's library with a raised loggia, pulled down 1969), Hollywood (1906 grant, Marsh and Russell's Tudor cottage, the only California Carnegie moved from its first site, pulled down 1958), Imperial (1909 grant, W. H. Weeks, wrecked in the 1940 El Centro earthquake), Los Gatos (1901 grant, built 1903, pulled down 1954), Monrovia (1905 grant, pulled down 1956), Ontario (1905 grant, F. P. Burnham, pulled down 1959): no source opened said where they stood.
- National City (1909 grant, pulled down 1954): Wikipedia's list says the civic center stands on its site, but no source opened gave the civic center's address.
- Los Angeles: the Boyle Heights branch (W. J. Dodd, pulled down 1974, later the Benjamin Franklin Branch) has no site in any source opened; the Vernon branch (Kysor and Biggar, built 1915, damaged in the 1971 earthquake, pulled down 1974) was on South Central Avenue, number not found.
- Huntington Beach: the Wikipedia list says the 1951 library was built on the Carnegie site; the library article puts the Carnegie at Walnut Avenue and 8th Street and the 1951 library at 525 Main Street. A local history page would settle which corner.

## Tips for the next session
- The cover document (Register reference 64500054) is long and its text is one big block: pull passages out round each town name with a short script rather than reading it whole. Its sections F (styles, with lists of what stands and what is gone in each) and the "current uses" summary near the end are the most useful.
- The Wikipedia API refused this environment as too many requests; the article pages themselves still opened with the page reader. Wikipedia's list page holds street addresses and points for most standing libraries and some gone ones.
- The Los Angeles Public Library's page on Andrew Carnegie and its libraries gives sites and dates for all six Los Angeles branches; Oakland Public Library's own branch pages give hours.
- Searching the Pacific Coast Architecture Database by several town names at once found nothing useful; search it one town at a time.
- Pictures: Commons is slow without a login, so picture choices for this batch are left for a later pictures pass.
