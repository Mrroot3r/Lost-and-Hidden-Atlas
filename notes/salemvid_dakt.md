## What this batch is
Seventeen standing places in Salem, taken from the names in one walking-tour video about the city (the video is a tip only; its record is `source/videos/20260828__TJq36gLStE.md`). Added: the Salem Athenaeum, the Salem Armory drill shed (the National Park Service's visitor center), the Old Granite Courthouse and Superior Court on Federal Street, the 1909 Registry of Deeds and Probate Court, Old Town Hall on Derby Square, Plummer Hall and the Daland House (the old Essex Institute), Greenlawn Cemetery with the Dickson Memorial Chapel, the Salem Public Library in the Bertram house, the Masonic Temple on Washington Street, Wesley Methodist Church, the rebuilt Pequot Mills (Shetland Park), the first Salem Normal School on Broad Street and its successor the Sullivan Building, the Salem Witch Museum in the old East Church, the 1898 YMCA, First Church in Salem and Salem Common. Sources: Wikipedia read as raw text (the Salem Register list, the buildings' articles, the Federal Street, Downtown Salem, Essex Institute and Salem Common district articles), the National Park Service's armory and Polish-Salem industry pages, Salem State University's archive blog and history page, the Athenaeum's, Wesley church's, First Church's, Witch Museum's and the library's own pages, Historic Salem's building list, the Friends of Greenlawn's chapel post, Lost New England and the Streets of Salem blog. Pins are street-address points from the address lookup, each looked at on the 2023 MassGIS aerial; the two Federal Street court entries are marked as the right block only, because the aerial shows several court buildings, old and new, and which roof is which could not be told. Not checked: any interior on the ground; Old Town Hall's and Greenlawn's hours (the City's pages answered 404); the library's hours were taken from a search result quoting its own site; whether the old court buildings are still used by the courts; what the 1982 fire left inside the Masonic Temple.
