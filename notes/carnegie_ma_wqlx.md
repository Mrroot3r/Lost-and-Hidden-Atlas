## What this batch is

Forty-seven Carnegie buildings in Massachusetts, part of the pass the owner asked for to put the buildings Andrew Carnegie and the Rockefellers paid for on the map. That is 42 of the 43 town and branch libraries on Wikipedia's list and the five college libraries Carnegie paid for (Mount Holyoke's Williston, Radcliffe's Schlesinger, Smith's Neilson, Tufts' Eaton Hall and Wellesley's Clapp). Springfield's Central Library was on the Atlas already and is not repeated. Before this batch the Atlas had no Massachusetts Carnegie library.

Twenty-nine are still libraries (25 public, 4 college; some much enlarged); fifteen stand with other uses (homes in Rockport, South Hadley and South Worcester; offices in Walpole, Lakeville, Granby, Lynn's Haywood branch and Reading's town hall; classrooms in Tufts' Eaton Hall; a heritage centre in Edgartown; a Greek Orthodox community centre in Springfield; a school cafeteria at Quinsigamond; Sharon, Dighton and Lynn's Houghton branch empty or waiting for a new use); three are gone (Saugus, Needham, New Marlborough).

Sources: Wikipedia's list and articles, New England Carnegies (necarnegies.com, a page per library; some pages are about 2005 and out of date on present use), Buildings of New England, SAH Archipedia, Lost New England, Waymarking, the libraries', towns' and colleges' own pages, and local papers (Daily Hampshire Gazette, Greenfield Recorder, Vineyard Gazette, Walpole Patch, The Week Today, Lynn's Daily Item), read with the web fetch tool. Hours are from the operators' own pages only. Pins are ArcGIS geocoder points for the street addresses, or OpenStreetMap points for campus buildings, all looked at on the MassGIS leaf-off aerial.

Not checked:
- Wikipedia's list is out of date for Edgartown (a heritage centre since 2016), Dighton (closed 2021; the library moved to 207 Main Street in 2025), Granby (town offices), Lakeville (town offices, not a café) and Sharon (the library moved in 2025).
- Present use of the old Haywood and Houghton branches in Lynn, Walpole's and Dighton's old libraries: the newest source found is years old or silent.
- Grant years disagree between Wikipedia's list and New England Carnegies for Athol, Berkley, Lynn, Reading, Leominster and Edgartown; the places give both.
- Saugus: only New England Carnegies says the 1998 library stands on the Carnegie site. Needham's address (968 Highland Avenue) is from New England Carnegies and the list only; the pin is "area". New Marlborough's new library may not be on the old spot; pin "area".
- Quinsigamond: the old library is built into the school; the pin is OpenStreetMap's school point ("site"). Its address is given three ways.
- Granby: the old library is under trees on the aerial; pin "site".
- Hours "?" for Berkley (its site refuses automated reading), Stoneham (its own pages disagree after 2025 cuts), Mount Holyoke and Smith (only one day's hours shown).
- Red brick: "yes" from a source's words (Rockland, South Hadley, Quinsigamond, Tufts, Smith) or a Commons picture looked at (Somerville East, Indian Orchard, Walpole, Radcliffe); "no" for stone, marble, granite, wood, buff, tan or yellow brick, from a source or a picture; "?" where nothing settled it (the tinted postcards of Hudson and Marlborough were not trusted).
- Pictures: 33 places have a Wikimedia Commons photograph chosen from the finder's sheets. None was found that plainly shows the building for Rockport, Saugus, Sharon, Somerville Central, Williston (Mount Holyoke), Ashland, Chelsea, Granby, Hudson, Needham, New Marlborough, Reading and the two Lynn branches.
