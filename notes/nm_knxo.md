## What this batch is
Fifteen places scattered across New Mexico, which had 16 places, all but Lake Valley in Lincoln County: Fort Union (Mora County), the Pecos mission church, the three Salinas mission ruins (Quarai, Abó, Gran Quivira), Fort Craig and San Miguel church in Socorro County, Fort Selden near Las Cruces, the silver camp of Mogollon, the Montezuma Castle and the Plaza Hotel at Las Vegas, the churches of Ranchos de Taos and Las Trampas, Dawson Cemetery (Colfax County) and Fort Bayard (Grant County). Sources: National Park Service pages read with `page.py` (park hours, directions, history pages and nps.gov/places), the BLM Fort Craig page, Register forms from NPGallery `_text` (Dawson Cemetery, Fort Bayard, Mogollon), Wikipedia with `wiki_raw.py`, the San Miguel parish schedule, the UWC-USA visit page, the Plaza Hotel's history page, New Mexico Magazine, KRWG, Atlas Obscura and newmexico.org. Pins were checked on Esri satellite; Fort Union, Pecos, Fort Selden and Mogollon were moved with `pix.py`. Not checked: nothing seen on the ground; Fort Selden's hours and fee (the state's page gave none); Ranchos de Taos and Plaza Hotel hours (no page found); Las Trampas opening times; Fort Bayard's museum hours (only a 2014 news story and Atlas Obscura); who owns the road and ground at Dawson Cemetery; parking at the standing places in towns. Fort Selden's pin is the walled rectangle seen on the aerial next to the mapped lot, taken to be the fort (the land line agrees: Fort Selden State Park). Mogollon and Fort Bayard are area pins. Red brick read from one photograph (Plaza Hotel); the Montezuma Castle photograph shows pinkish walls that look like stone, left '?'.
