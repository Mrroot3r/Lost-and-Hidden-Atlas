## What this batch is

Thirty of New Jersey's Carnegie libraries, part of the pass the owner asked for to put on the map the buildings Andrew Carnegie (and the Rockefellers) paid for. The list worked from is Wikipedia's "List of Carnegie libraries in New Jersey" (36 libraries, grants of 1900 to 1917), checked against Dubicki's article "Carnegie Libraries in New Jersey: 1900-1923" (New Jersey Studies, 2017), which has a table of grants and opening years. Before this batch the Atlas had none of them.

Added: still libraries at Avon, Bayonne, Belleville, Belmar, Edgewater, Elizabeth (main), Freehold, Kearny, Long Branch, Montclair's Bellevue Avenue branch, New Brunswick, Nutley, Perth Amboy and Verona; standing with other uses at Atlantic City (Carnegie Library Center), Caldwell (closed since the 2021 flood), Camden (the main library, an empty ruin), East Orange (the municipal court, and the Elmwood and Franklin branches), Elizabeth's Liberty Square branch (senior center), Lakewood (see below), Montclair (the Unitarian church's annex), Vineland (senior center), West Hoboken (the Musto Cultural Center, Union City) and Westfield (offices); and the sites of Collingswood's, Cranford's and Union Hill's (Union City; torn down by March 2026). Also the Cooper Library in Johnson Park, Camden, which the Wikipedia list counts as a Carnegie branch but whose National Register nomination says was Eldridge Johnson's gift; its text says so.

Sources: Wikipedia as raw text; the libraries' own pages (hours, histories), National Register nominations, the New Jersey Historic Trust, Preservation New Jersey, HMdb, borough and city pages and local papers, read with the web fetch tool. Hours are from each operator's own page; directories were not used. Pins are ArcGIS geocoder points for street addresses, or OpenStreetMap points, each looked at on the leaf-off aerial.

Not checked: Lakewood's pin is 200 Monmouth Avenue, given in a directory for Catholic Charities' office; the Ocean County Library says the old library on Monmouth Avenue houses Catholic Charities, while the Wikipedia list and NJ Monthly say it was torn down, so the place is marked as mixed. Whether East Orange's Elmwood branch is open, and what the Franklin branch is used for now. What the Atlantic City building is used for since 2022. Whether Caldwell's repairs have been done. Bayonne's hours (two schedules, unlabelled) and the Musto Center's. Red brick is "yes" for Edgewater (National Register form), Kearny (a newspaper), Perth Amboy and Verona (their Commons pictures); "some" for Camden's main library, Belleville (the 1981 front) and Montclair's old library (a brick band over stucco); "no" for stone, stucco and buff brick; "?" elsewhere.

Pictures: from Wikimedia Commons for the East Orange Elmwood branch, Long Branch, Verona and the Musto Center (West Hoboken); fourteen more places show their Wikipedia article's picture. None fitted East Orange's main and Franklin buildings, Avon, Caldwell, Elizabeth's Liberty Square branch, Lakewood, Nutley, Vineland or Westfield. The finder ran without the Commons login, at the slow pace.

## Tips

- Dubicki's article (New Jersey Studies 3, 2017, free PDF on njs.libraries.rutgers.edu) has a table of every New Jersey grant with dates, amounts, opening years and whether each building is still a library; better than the Wikipedia list, whose "razed" marks come from a 1996 walking club survey.
- The Wikipedia list is wrong in places: Union Hill was torn down in 2026; Orange's 348 Main Street is the Stickler library, not Carnegie's; Lakewood may stand.
- Lookalikes: Wikipedia's "Union City Public Library" is Union City, Indiana; lakewoodhistory.org is Lakewood, Ohio; littlefallshistoricalsociety.org is Little Falls, New York.
- Wikipedia's point for Camden's main library is a block off; OpenStreetMap maps the ruin as a building.
