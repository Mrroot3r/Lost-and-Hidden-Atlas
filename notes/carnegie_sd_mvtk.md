## What this batch is

Carnegie libraries of South Dakota (20 places), all still standing: town libraries, museums, offices and an arts centre, and the University of South Dakota's library, now the National Music Museum. The sources are Wikipedia's state list and articles, National Register nominations, South Dakota History's 1990 article on the state's Carnegie libraries and a 2021 article on Vermillion's two, and the libraries', cities' and museums' own pages. Sioux Falls was already on the map.

## What was not checked

- Hours were found on the operators' own pages only for Armour, Deadwood, Mitchell and Watertown; the rest are `hours='?'`. A page giving Madison's hours sits on a web firm's domain, so it was not used.
- What Dallas, Rapid City, Sisseton and Yankton are used for now was not found (the 1990 article says Rapid City was a design firm and Yankton a restaurant).
- Pins on the road or under trees are 'area': Canton, Dallas, Rapid City, Sisseton, Madison, the National Music Museum, Yankton (northwest corner of Fourth and Capitol). Hot Springs, Tyndall and Wessington Springs each have two addresses in the sources.
- Mitchell's walls: the 1990 article was read only in summary, so they are left `brick='?'`.
- Private owners named in the Hot Springs, Sisseton, Vermillion and Yankton sources are left out.
- Lake Andes and Sisseton stand inside reservation boundaries; both are town libraries.

## Leads not finished

- Aberdeen (1902 to 1950), Huron (1909 to 1965) and Britton (1921 to 2002, torn down for a school): sites not found.
- Pierre (burned 1994, torn down 1995): stood just east of the courthouse; street address not found.
- Wagner: standing, built to George Issenhuth's plans, but its address was not found.
- Yankton College's Carnegie library (1903) stands inside the federal prison camp on the old campus; not added, as visitors cannot go in.
