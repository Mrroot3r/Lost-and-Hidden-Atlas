## What this batch is
Fourteen places in Douglas County, Kansas, in the Kansas City area (Lawrence and Eudora): the Riggs, Usher, Goodrich, McCurdy, Miller, Zimmerman, Stephens, French, Bell, Mugan-Olmsted and Waters houses and the Pilla House in Eudora, the Old West Lawrence Historic District, and the former First Methodist Church of Eudora. The Atlas had 88 places within 22 km of Lawrence, but none of these. They were found by comparing the Register list for Douglas County with the built place list by name (the first two Douglas batches had taken the public buildings and left the houses). Sources: National Register nomination forms from NPGallery for the Riggs, Usher, Goodrich, McCurdy, Miller, Zimmerman, Stephens, Pilla, Bell and Old West Lawrence listings (read with page.py; each of these tool outputs stops at about 12,000 characters, so long forms were read only in part), the Kansas Historical Society's register summaries for the French, Mugan-Olmsted and Waters houses (no form was read for them), and Wikipedia for the Eudora church. Coordinates are ArcGIS street-address points looked at on the Kansas 2024 aerial; all fourteen pins fall on a building (the Old West Lawrence pin is the district's middle and marks an area). Pictures: seven have one from Commons (Riggs, Usher, Goodrich, Stephens, Bell, Pilla, Old West Lawrence) and the Eudora church uses its Wikipedia picture; McCurdy, Zimmerman, Miller, French, Mugan-Olmsted and Waters have none. Red brick: yes for Riggs and McCurdy (forms) and the Eudora church (a Wikipedia photograph, looked at); some for Zimmerman (first story) and the Old West district; no for Usher, Goodrich, Bell, Mugan-Olmsted and Pilla (stone or wood by the forms) and Stephens (a Commons photograph shows its brick painted cream); not stated ('?') for Miller, French and Waters. All are private houses, so every one is look-from-the-street; owners, interiors and today's condition were not checked. The Bell House date is not in the part of the form that was read (a lot bought in October 1862 and a family in it in August 1863). The Pilla form says 1854 once, against a newspaper of May 1894 quoted in the same form: the entry gives 1894. The Stephens form puts the house northwest of town but the Kansas Historical Society gives 340 North Michigan Street; the pin is at the address and Commons photographs of the house within 41 m confirm it. Nothing seen on the ground.

## Tips
- Checking a Register county list against the map by name, not only by distance, finds what a distance test hides: in Lawrence a district pin within 250 m made twenty houses look covered. A short script that prints rows whose names are not in `build/sites.json` is faster than `start.py` for a county that has had several batches.
- Douglas County is the only county of the four Kansas ones in the Kansas City brief where houses were still missing. Leavenworth, Johnson and Wyandotte Register lists are nearly all on the Atlas, so a session sent there should work non-Register sources (the Kansas Historical Society's survey reports; Fort Leavenworth's own pages) or take Missouri.
- `page.py` on an NPGallery text prints at most about 12,000 characters, so a 150-page district form reads only its front matter; for those, the Kansas Historical Society summary is all that could be read.
- Kansas Register forms from the 2020s (Fox House, Eudora Methodist and others) are not on NPGallery as text; the Kansas Historical Society lists them with a short summary and a PDF link whose name is in the page's HTML.
- ArcGIS `findAddressCandidates` with `countryCode=USA` returned PointAddress matches at score 100 for every Lawrence and Eudora address tried.
