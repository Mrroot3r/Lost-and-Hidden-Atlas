## What this batch is

Carnegie libraries of Nebraska, towns M to Z (28 places): 25 standing buildings and 3 sites of torn-down ones (Randolph, Schuyler, South Omaha), from Wikipedia's state list, the 2018 National Register document "Carnegie Libraries in Nebraska", single nominations, History Nebraska and the operators' own sites. Hours only from the operators' pages. With the A to L batch this covers 61 of the state's 69 Carnegie libraries; the rest are leads below.

## What was not checked

- Present use was not found for Madison, North Bend, Pawnee City, Ravenna, Seward, Stromsburg, Superior, Tecumseh, University Place and Wayne; all are set to look from the street.
- Hours not found on the operators' own sites for the Museum of the High Plains (McCook), the West Nebraska Arts Center (Scottsbluff), O'Neill, Ponca (only a 2020 post) and Spencer.
- Norfolk: a November 2025 news item says a "historic library in Norfolk" will become rentals; it does not say the Carnegie building.
- Shelton: a 2024 National Register nomination is on History Nebraska's site; whether it was listed was not confirmed.
- Two addresses for Madison, Ponca, Seward and Wayne; Madison, Stromsburg, Wayne and Wymore pins are 'area'.
- The Nebraska Library Commission's per-library pages (nlc.nebraska.gov/history/carnegie/) refused this environment; they would likely settle most present uses.

## Leads not finished

- Pierce (1912): the building was moved by volunteers and is now the Carnegie Event Center, run by the Pierce County Foundation; its new location was not found. Original site 200 East Willow Street.
- Sutton (1910 or 1911, condemned 1969, torn down): it stood on Saunders Avenue north of today's library at 201 South Saunders; the exact site was not found.
