## What this batch is
Twenty-one places in the eastern and middle parts of Washington County, New York (region `wash`, drawn at random by `choose.py --state ny`, which offered Hartford, Cambridge and Salem). The region's 27 places were nearly all on the Hudson and the old Champlain Canal; the farm towns to the east had only covered bridges. Almost all standing: in Salem the 1869 courthouse (now a community centre with its original courtroom), the Revolutionary War Cemetery with the 1778 stockade-fort marker, the village centre along Main Street and Broadway, and the Asa Fitch house and "Bug House" laboratory (pin is the roadside marker, not the house); in Cambridge Hubbard Hall (1878 opera house) and the village centre (St. Luke's Tiffany interior, Rice Seed buildings); White Creek hamlet; in Hartford the Baptist church with the footings of its 1813 predecessor, the museum and Civil War enlistment centre, and Brown's Tavern (1802) at South Hartford; in Granville the South Granville Congregational Church, the Lemuel Haynes House (National Historic Landmark, private), the Slate Valley Museum, the town hall and post office, and Middle Granville with the Dayton-Williams House (pin is the hamlet marker); the Easton Friends North Meetinghouse (1838), the Fort Miller Reformed Church (1816), the Hebron Valley Grange, Whitehall's Main Street and armory, and Greenwich village with its Underground Railroad houses. Sources: Wikipedia read as raw text with `wiki_raw.py` (the county Register list and each article), HMdb marker pages (nearby lists, 4-mile radius, round seven towns), and with `page.py` the Town of Salem's landmark tour and cemetery pages, Lakes to Locks Passage pages for the Salem courthouse and the Slate Valley Museum, the Pomeroy Foundation's courthouse marker page, Hubbard Hall's visit and history pages, the Town of Hartford's museum page, the Slate Valley Museum's visit page, the Easton Library's Quaker page, New York Yearly Meeting's Easton page and the state military museum's Whitehall Armory page. Not checked: anything on the ground; interiors; hours of the churches, the Fort Miller church's present use, the Hebron grange's owner and use, and whether the Whitehall Athletic Club admits visitors; parking for most places (few lots are mapped in these hamlets). Six pins were moved or replaced after the aerial check (see the pin notes): the Wikipedia points for the Fitch house, the Dayton-Williams house and the Lemuel Haynes house fell in fields, and the Haynes pin is on the nearest house that fits the description, not a confirmed one. Brick: six settled from Wikipedia photographs (Hartford Baptist, Easton meetinghouse, Dayton-Williams, Whitehall armory and Main Street red brick; Hubbard Hall is frame), seven left at '?'.

## Tips
- HMdb nearby lists with `Miles=4` reach well into Vermont from eastern Washington County and still return about a hundred markers each; a short loop over `page.py --all` cutting each marker page between "Inscription." and "Touch for map" read 35 markers in one go.
- The Town of Salem's landmark tour (`townofsalemny.gov/landmark-tour/`) gives date, address and fabric for every notable building in the Salem district, more than the Wikipedia article.
- Lakes to Locks Passage pages (`passageport.org/places-to-go/<name>/`) carry season, hours, fees and the managing body for heritage sites in Washington County; `page.py -g` skips the long menu.
- Four Wikipedia coordinates in this batch were off the building (Fitch, Dayton-Williams and Haynes in fields, Hartford Baptist on the road); the ArcGIS PointAddress for "58 East Broadway, Salem" fell on a house 100 m from the courthouse, where `osmnear.py ... buildings` found the courthouse by its OpenStreetMap name.
- `publish.sh` stopped twice in ten minutes because other batches merged; after `resync.sh` only `build_sites.py` and a fresh batch time were needed before publishing again.
