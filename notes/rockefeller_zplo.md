## What this batch is

This batch adds 28 buildings and works outside New York that were paid for by John D. Rockefeller, his son John D. Rockefeller Jr., or the funds they set up. It is the last Rockefeller batch of the Carnegie and Rockefeller pass the owner asked for. The New York ones are in `rockefeller_ny_sypl`.

- **Pennsylvania:** Bryn Mawr's Rockefeller Hall.
- **Rhode Island:** Brown's Faunce House (built as Rockefeller Hall).
- **Maine:** the Acadia carriage roads and their stone bridges, and the Brown Mountain Gate Lodge.
- **Ohio:**
  - the Rockefeller Building in Cleveland;
  - the site of Euclid Avenue Baptist Church, torn down;
  - the Rockefeller Park bridges;
  - the Heights Rockefeller Building;
  - the Rockefeller Physics Building at Case.
- **Illinois:** at the University of Chicago, Rockefeller Chapel, Harper Library and International House.
- **California:** International House Berkeley and the Yosemite Museum.
- **Arizona:** the Yavapai museum at the Grand Canyon.
- **Wyoming:** the Norris, Madison and Fishing Bridge museums in Yellowstone.
- **Georgia:** Spelman's Sisters Chapel.
- **Tennessee:**
  - Fisk's Cravath Hall;
  - the Rockefeller Memorial at Newfound Gap, which stands on the Tennessee to North Carolina line.
- **Virginia:** in Williamsburg, the Wren Building, the Governor's Palace and Bassett Hall.
- **Florida:** The Casements.
- **New Jersey:** the site of Golf House in Ocean County Park, marked.
- **Ontario:** Toronto's FitzGerald Building.
- **Quebec:** the Montreal Neurological Institute.

Sources: the National Park Service; college and university pages; the Encyclopedia of Cleveland History; Cleveland Historical; and Wikipedia read as raw text. Pins come from the ArcGIS geocoder, OpenStreetMap outlines or Wikipedia coordinates, and each was looked at on the aerial. Places that already had a place list entry were left out: the Capitol at Williamsburg, and Philipsburg Manor in New York.

Not checked:
- Opening hours, from the operator's own page, for Rockefeller Chapel, the Yosemite Museum, the three Yellowstone museums, the Governor's Palace, Bassett Hall and The Casements. All are `hours='?'`; the park museums are seasonal.
- How much of the Rockefeller Park bridges Rockefeller's 1896 gift to the Park Board paid for. Cleveland Historical says it partly funded them.
- The Newfound Gap memorial's link to the Rockefellers is weaker than the others'. It honours the Laura Spelman Rockefeller Memorial's $5 million gift for park land, and the stonework itself was built by the Civilian Conservation Corps.

Red brick:
- **Yes, from their Commons pictures:** Faunce House, the Rockefeller Building, the Heights Rockefeller Building, the Physics Building, Cravath Hall and the Governor's Palace.
- **No:** Acadia's roads and bridges, the Rockefeller Park bridges and Yavapai, which are stone.
- **?:** everything else. The FitzGerald Building's brick is brown in its picture.

Pictures:

## Tips

- Many Rockefeller buildings are named for him without his money, or the other way round. Read the gift in a source.
- The park museums of the 1920s (Yosemite, Grand Canyon, Yellowstone) were paid for by grants from the Laura Spelman Rockefeller Memorial (for Yosemite, given through the American Association of Museums). Look for that fund's name, not the family's.
