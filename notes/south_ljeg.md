## What this batch is
Seventeen places across Plymouth County, where the Atlas had nothing within 15 km of Kingston: in Plymouth, the Parting Ways settlement cemetery, Burial Hill, the Sparrow, Jabez Howland and Harlow houses, the Plimoth Grist Mill on the Jenney mill site and the Gurnet Light inside Fort Andrew; in Duxbury, the Myles Standish house site and monument, the Alden House with its trail to the 1630 first-house foundation, and the King Caesar House; the Isaac Winslow House and Hatch Mill in Marshfield, the 1706 Pembroke Friends Meetinghouse, the 1681 Old Ship Meetinghouse in Hingham, Oliver Mill Park (Peter Oliver's ironworks) in Middleborough and the Bridgewater Iron Works park. Sources were Wikipedia read with `wiki_raw.py`, the Pembroke Friends Register form, HMdb markers and the managing bodies' pages read with `page.py` (alden.org, duxburyhistory.org, plymouthantiquarian.org, winslowhouse.org, oldshipchurch.org, plimoth.org, plymouth-ma.gov, nsrwa.org). Not checked: hours for the Sparrow House, the Myles Standish Monument (DCR page not read), the Pembroke meetinghouse, Hatch Mill tour dates, Oliver Mill Park and Iron Works Park; the Howland Society page answered 403, so its season is Wikipedia's. Who owns the Parting Ways cemetery ground is not named in any source read (PAD-US holds it as open access). No brick picture was opened: brick is 'no' only where a source names granite, wood or earth, '?' elsewhere. Pins: Myles Standish house site moved to the OpenStreetMap cellar-hole point; Parting Ways is prec area (graves not seen under the pines); the Monument tower, the Iron Works storehouse and the Hatch Mill building were not picked out on the aerial. No pin marks only a trailhead. Nothing was checked on the ground.
