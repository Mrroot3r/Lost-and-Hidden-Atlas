## What this batch is

The 28 Carnegie branch libraries of Brooklyn (21) and Queens (7), the fourth batch of the pass the owner asked for (buildings Andrew Carnegie and the Rockefellers paid for), from Wikipedia's "List of Carnegie libraries in New York City". With `carnegie_nyc_fxbk` this covers every Carnegie library in the city.

Standing and still libraries (22): Arlington, Bedford, Brownsville (closed for renovation), Bushwick, Carroll Gardens (closed for renovation in 2023), DeKalb, Eastern Parkway, Flatbush (refaced in WPA Moderne in the 1930s), Fort Hamilton, Leonard, Macon, Pacific (the first in Brooklyn), Park Slope, Saratoga, Stone Avenue (built as one of the first children's libraries), Walt Whitman, Washington Irving (the last in Brooklyn, 1923) and Williamsburgh in Brooklyn; Astoria, Poppenhusen, Richmond Hill and Woodhaven in Queens. Gone, as sites (6): Greenpoint, Red Hook and South (Sunset Park) in Brooklyn; Elmhurst, Far Rockaway (burned 1962) and Flushing in Queens.

Sources: the Landmarks Preservation Commission's designation reports, the Historic Districts Council's Carnegie library survey pages, the Brooklyn Public Library's branch history pages, the city's design and construction office, 6 to Celebrate, Wikipedia and local news, read with the web fetch tool. Pins are geocoder points for the street addresses, each looked at on the leaf-off aerial.

Not checked: opening hours (none taken; "?" everywhere); whether Brownsville and Carroll Gardens have reopened and whether Bushwick closed in June 2026 as planned; the Red Hook building's street number (its pin is on Visitation Place, site precision); the Far Rockaway architect; landmark status for Carroll Gardens and Fort Hamilton; Pacific's opening year (1903 or 1904); which Queens branch was finished first (Far Rockaway or Astoria). Red brick is from the sources' own words: "yes" where they name red brick, "some" for Bedford (red and grey) and Astoria (saffron, with a red-brick entrance bay), "no" for limestone, light, saffron or yellow brick.

Pictures: chosen on Wikimedia Commons for all 22 standing buildings; the six sites have none. Brownsville (tan brick), Bushwick, Carroll Gardens, Park Slope and Saratoga (red brick) have their brick from those pictures.
