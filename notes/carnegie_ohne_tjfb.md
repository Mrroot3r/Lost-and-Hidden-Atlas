## What this batch is

Thirty-five Carnegie buildings in northeast Ohio, part of the pass the owner asked for to put the buildings Andrew Carnegie and the Rockefellers paid for on the map: Cleveland's branches (thirteen standing, the Woodland branch and South Brooklyn's library gone), East Cleveland, Lakewood, Akron, Alliance (gone), Ashtabula, Bristolville, Kent, Kinsman, Geneva, Madison, Salem, Warren, Willoughby, Youngstown, Wellsville, Steubenville, Lorain, Amherst, Wooster (gone) and Oberlin College's Carnegie building. Canton, Columbus Main and Zanesville's John McIntire library were already on the map and are left alone.

Sources: Wikipedia's list of Carnegie libraries in Ohio and its articles, the Encyclopedia of Cleveland History, Cleveland Historical, the City of Cleveland's landmark pages, National Register nominations, Waymarking, a history of Cleveland of 1910, and the libraries', historical societies' and colleges' own pages, read with the web fetch tool. Hours are from the operators' own pages only. Pins are ArcGIS geocoder points for street addresses, or OpenStreetMap's point for the Oberlin building, looked at on the Esri satellite aerial (Ohio has no state leaf-off imagery wired in).

Not checked:
- Cleveland Public Library's own site (cpl.org) put up a bot check in the browser and was left alone. The branch hours come from earlier fetches of its pages, which give two patterns (Brooklyn and Jefferson open to 7 three days a week; South and Sterling Mon–Sat 10–6); Carnegie West has none; Lorain's are from a page older than its 2023 reopening.
- Present use of the old East 79th, Miles Park, Quincy and Superior branches and the St. Clair building: the newest sources are about 2008. All are "look from the street".
- Geneva: the Western County Court's lease ends in September 2026 and a news report says it will move to Jefferson; whether it has gone was not found.
- Willoughby and Lakewood: how much of the 1909 and 1916 buildings is inside today's libraries. Both pins are "area".
- Wellsville and Steubenville: no hours on a page read. Wellsville: how much of the 1917 building shows after the 1998 and 1999 work. A Wellsville library site at wellsville-pl.ploud.net is another state's Wellsville.
- South Brooklyn's library: only the old village is known, so the pin is an area pin, not the building.
- Alliance's site: what stands on the corner now; the pin is on the corner from the street names.
- Warren's law library and Youngstown's main library: hours not given (the law library's site would not load; Youngstown's pages gave only summer 2026 hours).
- Red brick: "yes" from a source's words or a picture; "no" for sandstone (Akron), grey or buff brick (Ashtabula, Kent) and the stone and white buildings; "?" where nothing settled it.
- Pictures: Akron and Kent show their Wikipedia articles' pictures. Wikimedia Commons photographs were chosen for Youngstown and Cleveland's Broadway, Brooklyn and Carnegie West branches; the finder's sheets for the rest of the batch were still being gathered when this was published (Commons is slow without a login) and come with a later batch.
