## What this batch is

Thirty Carnegie buildings in northwest and north central Ohio, part of the pass the owner asked for to put the buildings Andrew Carnegie and the Rockefellers paid for on the map: Toledo's five Carnegie branches (Kent's site is gone), Maumee, Wauseon, Napoleon, Defiance, Bryan, Paulding, Celina (gone), Delphos, Kenton, Carey, Fostoria, Bellevue, Clyde, Tiffin, Heidelberg University's Carnegie library, Upper Sandusky, Marion, Bellefontaine, Bucyrus, Galion, Mansfield, New London, Norwalk, Milan and Sandusky. Northeast Ohio is in `carnegie_ohne_tjfb`.

Sources: Wikipedia's list of Carnegie libraries in Ohio and its articles, the Toledo library's architecture site (tlcplarchitecture.omeka.net), Waymarking, the Toledo Blade and other local papers, historical societies, and the libraries' and colleges' own pages, read with the web fetch tool. Hours are from operators' own pages only. Pins are ArcGIS geocoder points for street addresses, looked at on the Esri satellite aerial.

Not checked:
- Toledo's Mott branch: whether anything of the 1918 building is inside the clinic at 1055 Dorr Street (the library says the Carnegie branch was enlarged; the Toledo Blade says the Dorr Street library was built in 1972).
- Toledo's Jermain branch: the church use rests on Waymarking alone, undated. Locke: present owner and use. South: whether the clinic is still there. Kent: what stands on the corner now.
- Jermain is one of the five Carnegie branches per Wikipedia and Waymarking; the Toledo Blade's timeline names Birmingham instead.
- Bellefontaine: whether Richwood Bank's branch has opened in the building; the bank's address (120 East Sandusky Avenue) is taken as the library's corner.
- Hours "?": Celina (not the Carnegie building), Fostoria, Galion and the Marion museum (sites would not load), Norwalk (its page dates from 2014).
- Red brick: "yes" for Napoleon (Waymarking says red brick); "no" for sandstone, limestone, granite, grey, white and yellow brick (Defiance, Bryan, Paulding, Carey, Fostoria, Bellevue, Clyde, Mansfield, Sandusky); "?" elsewhere. No picture was looked at for brick.
- Pictures: Commons pictures were chosen for the five places that had no Wikipedia picture (Jermain, Maumee, Marion, Norwalk, Sandusky); red brick on Jermain, Maumee and Norwalk and part brick on Marion was read off those pictures. The other 25 take their picture from Wikipedia or have none.
