## What this batch is

The 39 Carnegie branch libraries of the New York Public Library in Manhattan (26), the Bronx (9) and Staten Island (4), the third batch of the pass the owner asked for (buildings Andrew Carnegie and the Rockefellers paid for), from Wikipedia's "List of Carnegie libraries in New York City". Before this batch the Atlas had none of them, and almost nothing in Manhattan at all.

Standing and still libraries (30): in Manhattan 115th Street (Harry Belafonte), 125th Street, 67th Street, 96th Street, Aguilar, Chatham Square, Columbus, Epiphany, Fort Washington, Hamilton Grange, Harlem, Hudson Park, Muhlenberg, St. Agnes, Seward Park, Tompkins Square, Washington Heights, Webster, Yorkville and the old 135th Street branch, now part of the Schomburg Center; on Staten Island Port Richmond, St. George, Stapleton and Tottenville; in the Bronx Hunts Point (closed for renovation), Melrose, Morrisania, Mott Haven, Tremont and Woodstock. Standing, other use (3): Rivington Street (condominiums), Fordham (empty, city-owned), Kingsbridge (a preschool). Gone, as sites (6): 58th Street, Hamilton Fish Park, Riverside, St. Gabriel's Park, West 40th Street (torn down 2020) and High Bridge.

Sources: the Landmarks Preservation Commission's designation reports, the Historic Districts Council's Carnegie library survey pages, Wikipedia, Six to Celebrate, Landmark West, local news, and the library's own branch pages, read with the web fetch tool. Pins are ArcGIS geocoder points for the street addresses (PointAddress hits for every standing building), each looked at on the leaf-off aerial.

Not checked: opening hours for most branches. The library's branch pages read in November 2026 showed weeks from earlier years or wrongly said a reopened branch was closed, so hours are given only for St. George, Stapleton, Tremont and Woodstock, and "?" elsewhere. Also not checked: when the 58th Street and Hamilton Fish Park buildings came down; whether the West 40th Street front was put back on the Covenant House building; Stapleton's landmark status. Red brick is "yes" where a source names red brick walls, "some" where it is part of the front (125th Street's side wall, Rivington Street's upper floors, Tottenville's basement and quoins), "no" for the limestone fronts; Tremont and Fordham are "yes" from their Wikimedia Commons pictures.

Pictures: 115th Street, Fort Washington, Hamilton Grange and Yorkville show their own Wikipedia article's picture; 27 more were chosen on Wikimedia Commons. None fitted Kingsbridge (only the newer library and nearby churches came up); the old 135th Street branch was not looked for (its link is kept off the Schomburg Center's article on purpose), and the sites of torn-down branches have none.
