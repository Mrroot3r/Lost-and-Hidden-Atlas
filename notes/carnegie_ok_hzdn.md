## What this batch is

Carnegie libraries of Oklahoma (22 places): 16 standing buildings, among them the University of Oklahoma's Carnegie Building, and the sites of 6 torn-down ones (Chickasha, Enid, Miami, Oklahoma City, Ponca City, Tulsa). Guthrie's library was already on the map (carnegie-library-guthrie) and is not repeated. Sources: Wikipedia's state list and articles, the Encyclopedia of Oklahoma History and Culture, National Register nominations, and the libraries', cities', museums' and university's own pages. Hours only from the operators' own pages.

## What was not checked

- Muskogee: damaged by fire in 2022 and later listed for sale; its present state and owner were not settled.
- Frederick: the city page gives both 200 East and 200 West Grand and does not itself say the building is the Carnegie one (the state encyclopedia counts it among those still libraries).
- Wagoner, Woodward and Frederick have two addresses each in the sources; pins are 'area'.
- Oklahoma City: the library's history says the northeast corner of NW 3rd and Robinson, another history the northwest; pinned at the crossing.
- Ponca City and Tulsa: the year each came down rests on Wikipedia's list alone.
- Cordell and Shawnee museums: no hours found on an operator's page.
- Lawton: used for art classes; no visiting hours.
- Gateway to Oklahoma History showed a bot check and was not used.
- Private owners (Bartlesville's law firm) are left out.

## Leads not finished

- Bartlesville (opened 1913, now law offices): the sources give 7th and Osage or Adams Boulevard; not pinned.
- McAlester (325 East Grand, razed 1973): nothing beyond the list; not pinned.
