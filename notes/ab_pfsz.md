## What this batch is
Fifteen places in the Crowsnest Pass coal towns and east of them, the first real batch in Alberta (Bankhead was the only place before). Ruins: Leitch Collieries at Passburg, the Lille ghost town and its Bernard coke ovens, the Lime City kilns on the Frank Slide, and the International Coal and Coke Company coke ovens in Coleman. Traces: the Frank Slide over the buried end of Frank. Standing: the Bellevue Underground Mine (guided tours underground), the Old Hillcrest Cemetery with the 1914 disaster graves, Coleman's main street (national historic site, with the 1905 union hospital folded in), the old Coleman High School (Crowsnest Museum), the Alberta Provincial Police barracks (Picariello shooting, 1922), the Polish Hall, the Blairmore Courthouse, the Doukhobor Prayer Home at Lundbreck, and in Fort Macleod the Territorial Court House and the Empress Theatre. Sources: the Canadian Register of Historic Places statement for every designated place (historicplaces.ca, `page.py --all`; the character-defining elements say what survives); Wikipedia through `wiki_raw.py`; the Government of Alberta pages for Leitch Collieries and the Frank Slide Interpretive Centre (both read in full, with hours); the Bellevue Mine's and the Crowsnest Museum's own pages; the Town of Fort Macleod; the Alberta heritage blog RETROactive (Bellevue Mine); the Crowsnest Pass "Take a Hike" sheet and the Friends of the Frank Slide Centre's Lille hike; Waymarking pages quoting interpretive signs for the Coleman ovens and Lime City (positions too); CBC and Lethbridge News Now on the 2018 court house sale. Red brick: yes for the Coleman ovens (sign text), the Coleman high school and the Fort Macleod court house (Register) and the Empress (Wikipedia photograph); no for the Polish Hall (stucco over brick), the Doukhobor home and the slide; the rest left `?`. **Not checked:** anything on the ground; who owns the Lille townsite (the walk is published by two local bodies, but the entry is ask-first until an owner is found), the Lime City kilns, the Coleman ovens, the Hillcrest cemetery (the municipality's cemetery page answered 404), the Polish Hall, the Blairmore Courthouse, and the Fort Macleod court house since its 2018 sale; whether the 1971 Coleman processing plant still stands; interiors of the Blairmore Courthouse, the Polish Hall and the Doukhobor home. Pins that mark only an area: the Frank Slide (debris field), Lille (townsite, not the ovens), Coleman main street, the Doukhobor home (moved onto the street; the building was not identified), and the Lime City kilns (moved onto a squarish masonry outline in the trees that may be the kilns, not confirmed). The Coleman oven pin was moved 150 m from the Waymarking point onto a long bank south of the railway seen on the aerial. There is no 1 m LiDAR and no public-land layer for Alberta, so every pin printed CHECK ACCESS; each access note names the owner where a source gave it.
