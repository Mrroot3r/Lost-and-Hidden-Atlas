## What this batch is

30 places: 26 standing, 4 gone (Eugene, Grants Pass, Klamath Falls, Ontario). Public libraries at Albany, Ashland, Coos Bay (Marshfield), Dallas, Enterprise, Gresham, Hermiston, Hillsboro, Hood River, La Grande, McMinnville, Medford, Milton-Freewater, Newberg, Oregon City, Pendleton, Salem, The Dalles, Union, Woodburn and Portland's Albina, Arleta, East Portland, North Portland and St. Johns branches; the academic one at Pacific University in Forest Grove. Baker City's Carnegie library was already on the Atlas (crossroads-carnegie-baker) and was not added again.

Sources: Wikipedia's state list and articles, the Oregon Encyclopedia, Register records and nominations (Enterprise, Arleta, Coos Bay, Pendleton, La Grande), the libraries', museums', arts centers' and cities' own pages, Newberg's 2012 Carnegie passport, the East Oregonian, Waymarking, HMdb.

## What was not checked

- Pins were looked at on the aerial. Eugene, Grants Pass, Hillsboro and Klamath Falls are street-corner points (area); McMinnville, Newberg, Oregon City, Woodburn and Albina sit in libraries with large additions (site).
- Use now not found for Dallas (a children's center in 2009), Hillsboro, Coos Bay (sold to a private owner in 1985), Medford (city offices in 2009) and Hermiston (reuse undecided in 2025).
- Hours not found or not checked for Ashland, Enterprise, Hood River, La Grande, McMinnville, St. Johns, Union and Woodburn. Gresham's museum gives only a closure and its events page.
- Brick: red only for Enterprise, La Grande, Arleta and East Portland; stucco or plaster at Ashland, Coos Bay, Pendleton and Albina. The rest are '?'.
- Dates disagree for Eugene's grant (1903 or 1906), Grants Pass (grant 1903, or a 1904 offer refused and 1920 accepted), Gresham's closing (1989 or 1990), Hermiston's grant (1915 or 1916), Hood River (built 1912 or 1913–14), Klamath Falls (replaced 1926 or 1928), Newberg's addition (1984–85 or 1986) and Ontario's demolition (1964 or 1967); both are given.
