## What this batch is
Eighteen standing places in region `troy` (drawn at random by `choose.py --state ny`). The region had one place in Lansingburgh (Rensselaer Park, gone) and its Troy entries were mostly vanished downtown sites and the South Troy iron works, so this batch is the standing buildings. Lansingburgh: the Herman Melville House (Lansingburgh Historical Society), the 1820 Lansingburgh Academy (now the library branch), the Village Burial Ground (1771), Trinity Church Lansingburgh with its 1807 graveyard, the First Presbyterian Church, the 1849 Methodist Episcopal church (Joy of Troy SDA), the Lion Factory (1884 collar works, apartments since 2024) and the c. 1720 "Old House" at 580 First Avenue. Troy: St. Paul's Episcopal (whole Tiffany interior, 1891–93), the Church of the Holy Cross (A. J. Davis nave, 1844; RPI-owned since 2009), the Cannon Building, West Hall (Old Troy Hospital) and the Winslow Laboratory at RPI, the Kate Mullany House, the main Troy Public Library (Tiffany window), the United Waste "Fortress" warehouse in South Troy, Washington Park (private square) and the Emma Willard School's Mount Ida campus. Sources: Wikipedia through `wiki_raw.py` (and `--rows` on the Rensselaer County Register list); Register forms 16000412 and 13000054 from NPGallery; the Lansingburgh Historical Society's pages; HMdb 313522; the library's hours page; St. Paul's, Trinity Lansingburgh and Kate Mullany NHS pages; the National Trust page for Mullany; the governor's 2022 nominations release; the City of Troy Lansingburgh district leaflet; an Albany Business Review article (2024, PDF copy) on the Lion Factory apartments. Not checked: anything on the ground; interiors except St. Paul's, the Methodist church (form) and the libraries; the Melville House's hours (the society's page gives none); the burial ground's owner and gate rules; the First Presbyterian building's description and present congregation (only the historical society's newspaper extracts were read, so `conf='mixed'`); the "Old House" date, which rests on the historical society's page alone (`conf='mixed'`); the Holy Cross and Fortress present uses. Pins are on the buildings (geocoder PointAddress or OpenStreetMap building points), all looked at on the leaf-off aerial; the Fortress pin was moved 100 m west from Wikipedia's point onto the OpenStreetMap building at 1 Jackson Street. Red brick: six set to `no` from Wikipedia photographs (Melville House white clapboard, Academy painted buff, Trinity brown stone, Cannon Building painted, West Hall painted cream); the rest `?` because Wikimedia refused further pictures (HTTP 429).
