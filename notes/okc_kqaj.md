## What this batch is
Fourteen standing buildings in Oklahoma City, taken from the names in one walk-through video (Old World Exploration, "Old World Oklahoma City"; the record of every name is `source/videos/20240212_MWCPHIr8mJw.md`). Downtown: St. Joseph Old Cathedral, First Christian Church on Robinson (Frontline Church), the First National Bank and Trust tower, the Skirvin Hotel, the India Temple Shrine Building (The Heritage and the national memorial museum), the Oklahoma Publishing Company Building, the Pioneer Telephone Building, Central High School (now the OCU law school), the 1937 county courthouse, the 1937 Municipal Building and the 1912 post office and courthouse; uptown, Taft Junior High School, Oklahoma City University's historic campus and the Governor's Mansion. Facts from Wikipedia read as raw text (`wiki_raw.py`) and from Register forms on NPGallery (07000521 Municipal Building, 74001665 post office, 07000515 Taft, 78002247 OCU), all of which read as text. Hours from St. Joseph's parish page, Frontline Church's downtown page, the National Park Service's memorial hours page and the governor's office mansion page, read November 23, 2026. Not checked: parking for any of them (downtown lots and garages, all private; no `th=` given), the Skirvin's hours, the courthouse's and city hall's public hours, the OCU law school's museum hours, whether the First National banking hall can be visited, and every interior. Pins: six address lookups or OpenStreetMap points sit on the buildings; Taft was moved onto the school (building work round it on the aerial); OCU's Register point fell on apartments and is now Wikipedia's campus point with `prec='area'`; the Publishing Company pin marks only the corner of Broadway and NW 4th, where OpenStreetMap names a "Buick Building". ## Tips
- Oklahoma County's Register forms on NPGallery all read as text with `page.py`; the OCU campus form's OCR spells the Gold Star building "Goldbug".
- The National Register list point for Oklahoma City University (35.494983, -97.544106) lands on campus apartments, not the historic buildings.
- A web search for the Oklahoma City livestock exchange building turned up an Oklahoman report that it burned in 1980; the stockyards' historic district still stands.
