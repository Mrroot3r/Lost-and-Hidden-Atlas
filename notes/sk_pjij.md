## What this batch is
Fifteen places in southeast Saskatchewan, the second Saskatchewan batch. Places: the Claybank Brick Plant (the lead the sk_fwmi note left); the Motherwell Homestead and Christ Anglican Church at Abernethy; at Fort Qu'Appelle, the surviving Hudson's Bay Company post building (the town museum) and the 1911 Grand Trunk Pacific station; the Sacred Heart church and the Stations of the Cross at Lebret; All Saints at Katepwa Beach; St. Peter's pro-cathedral and the 1906 town hall at Qu'Appelle; Wolseley's 1893–95 court house and its town hall/opera house; the Cannington Manor village site; the Fort Espérance site; the Esterhazy Flour Mill; and the Wood End NWMP post in Estevan. Every place has its Canadian Register statement (historicplaces.ca, read whole with `page.py --all`; the character-defining elements say what survives). Other sources: Wikipedia via `wiki_raw.py`, Parks Canada visit pages (Motherwell, Fort Espérance), the Museums Association of Saskatchewan listings (Claybank, Fort Qu'Appelle, Esterhazy, Estevan), claybankbrick.ca, the National Trust's Claybank page (via canadashistory.ca), the Town of Wolseley and Town of Esterhazy pages, Tourism Saskatchewan listings, the Sask Parks Cannington Manor driving-tour PDF, the Encyclopedia of Saskatchewan, and the Catholic Register and Grasslands News on Lebret. Red brick: Claybank's stacks are buff brick in the Wikipedia photograph, so 'no'. Five places are '?': the Fort Qu'Appelle post building, St. Peter's (the register says "brick" but gives no colour), the two Wolseley buildings (the same) and Fort Espérance. **Not checked:** hours at Motherwell (Parks Canada's hours page is drawn by script; only "May to September" was read), Cannington Manor (the hours come from a 2021 news report; the Sask Parks page is drawn by script), Fort Espérance, the churches, the Fort Qu'Appelle station and the Qu'Appelle town hall. Interiors were not checked except where the register lists them. Parking is mapped only at Motherwell; the town places use street parking, and nothing was found for Claybank, Cannington Manor or Fort Espérance. **Pins that mark only an area or could not be confirmed on the aerial:** Fort Espérance (register point, open field); All Saints Katepwa (trees at a road corner); St. Peter's and the Qu'Appelle town hall (address points; the aerial is too coarse to pick out the buildings); Wood End (address point beside a larger building). Sources disagree on: Claybank's designation year (1994 in the register, 1996 in Wikipedia); the Cannington Manor church (1884 in the register, 1885 in the Encyclopedia's photo caption); Fort Espérance's dates and designation year; the Lebret mission's founding year (1865 or 1866); the Wolseley court house year (1893–95 or 1894); and the Wolseley opera house number (510 or 610 Varennes). Nothing was checked on the ground.

## Tips
- The Wikipedia "List of historic places in Saskatchewan" splits on `{{HPC row` like Alberta's. Its rows have no `location=`, so filter by `lat=`/`lon=` inside a box. Regina, Moose Jaw and Saskatoon are not in it (they have their own lists).
- The historicplaces.ca text that `page.py --all` keeps gives each place's address right after `Share | -->`: `grep -o 'Share | --> \.[^.]*\.[^.]*'`.
- Register list coordinates in the Qu'Appelle valley towns were often 200 to 250 m off (Fort Qu'Appelle station, Qu'Appelle town hall, St. Peter's). `nom.py` found the station as "Fort Qu'Appelle railway station" and the Motherwell stone house as "Lanark Place, Abernethy" but not under their register names.
- Tourism Saskatchewan listing pages are mostly menu; the useful text is near the end of `page.py --all` output. Web search results gave titles only, no quoted text.
