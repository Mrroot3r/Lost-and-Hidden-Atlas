## What this batch is
Seventeen places in West Brookfield, Brookfield, North Brookfield and Warren (Worcester County), where the Atlas had nothing within 10 km of West Brookfield (region cmass drawn at random by choose.py). West Brookfield: the Lucy Stone Home Site (farmhouse ruins, The Trustees), the Jedediah Foster Homesite (foundations, Quaboag Historical Society park), the Quaboag Plantation markers on Foster Hill (Ayres' fortified house besieged in 1675; private land, view only), the Fort Gilbert marker, the Old Indian Cemetery, the Salem Cross Inn (White Homestead, c. 1707/1740), the 1760 Hitchcock Tavern, and the 1847 and 1884 railroad depots (Quaboag Historical Society museum). Brookfield: Brookfield Cemetery, Brookfield Common, the Merrick Public Library (Banister Memorial Hall) and Elm Hill Farm. North Brookfield: Camp Atwater and the Town House. Warren: the Crossman lenticular pony-truss bridge, the public library and the 1875 First Congregational (now Emmanuel Orthodox) church. Sources: Wikipedia with wiki_raw.py (Register list rows for Worcester County), HMdb nearby lists round five points, and page.py on the West Brookfield Historical Commission's site (westbrookfield.org, which reads cleanly and covers almost every West Brookfield site), The Trustees, the two libraries' own pages, the Salem Cross Inn and two articles. Not checked: nothing on the ground; hours for the Quaboag Historical Society museum (none found), Emmanuel church's services, the two cemeteries' hours (Brookfield's town page answers 403); whether Hitchcock Tavern is serving today (only an undated business-journal article on its reopening); whether any part of the North Brookfield Town House is open; where to park at the Lucy Stone and Foster sites. Pins: the Lucy Stone pin was moved onto a foundation seen on the LiDAR sheet; Elm Hill Farm's Register point was in woods and was moved to the nearest farm cluster on East Main Street (area, identity not confirmed). Red brick: the Merrick library from its own history page; Salem Cross Inn (brick chimney), the North Brookfield Town House and the Warren church from the Wikipedia photographs opened with brick.py; the Warren library (granite) and Crossman bridge from their articles; nine left '?'.

## Tips
- westbrookfield.org (West Brookfield Historical Commission) reads cleanly with page.py and has a page for most sites in the town; its address for the Old Indian Cemetery is spelled "old-indian-cemetary".
- warrenpubliclibrary.org/hours/ is reported as drawn by script, but page.py --all still prints the full hours table.
- eqlt.org (East Quabbin Land Trust) and brookfieldma.us answer 403 to page.py; the archived copies it names were not fetched in an unattended run.
- The Register list coordinates for Elm Hill Farm and Warren Town Hall fell in woods; the ArcGIS PointAddress results landed on the building every time an address was known.
