## What this batch is
Nineteen more places spread over Manitoba, which had 18 (batch mb_xums and the Old Pinawa Dam), so it now has 37. Winnipeg: Riel House, the Upper Fort Garry gate and the Grey Nuns' convent (Le Musee de Saint-Boniface Museum). Lower Red River and Interlake: St. Andrew's Rectory, the St. Andrews lock and Camere curtain dam at Lockport, the Stonewall Quarry Park lime kilns, the Rosehill lime kilns and Hecla Historic Village. South: Fort Dufferin, the Emerson town hall and court house, the Manitou Opera House, the Ninette Sanatorium and the Harrison Mill at Holmfield. West: Daly House in Brandon, Thomas Fuller's post office (now City Hall) at Portage la Prairie, the Fort Ellice site, and the Church of the Resurrection at Dauphin. North: the McArdle Salt Works by Highway 10 and The Pas court house (Sam Waller Museum). Facts and a site coordinate for each came from the Manitoba Historical Society's "Historic Sites of Manitoba" pages (`page.py --all -n 30000`), with the Province's heritage page for Dauphin, Wikipedia for Hecla, and the operators' own pages for hours (Parks Canada for Riel House and the Rectory, msbm.mb.ca, dalyhousemuseum.ca, samwallermuseum.ca, Manitoba Parks for Hecla, the Friends of Upper Fort Garry, Public Services and Procurement Canada for the lock, stonewallquarrypark.ca). Two local news reports: PembinaValleyOnline (2017) for who looks after Fort Dufferin, CTV News (2025) for the Ninette sale. Nothing was checked on the ground. Not checked: the Rectory's interior opening (Parks Canada now describes a self-guided tour of the grounds only); when Fort Dufferin's interpretive centre opens; the Stonewall interpretive centre's hours; the Manitou Opera House's own page (403); whether a car road reaches the Rosehill kilns (the pin is on a dirt track in woods, prec area, no kiln visible); who owns the McArdle Salt Works ground; whether NCC opens Fort Ellice (marked ask); the Saint-Boniface museum said "estimated reopening October 19th, 2026" on its page. Pins moved after the pin check to OpenStreetMap points: Riel House, the Saint-Boniface museum, the Rectory, Daly House (the MHS point was in 18th Street), the Manitou Opera House and Portage City Hall. The Sam Waller Museum pin is the MHS point at prec site (the roof could not be told). There is no 1 m LiDAR and no public-land layer in Manitoba, so every `CHECK ACCESS` line is expected; access notes name the owner from the sources. Red brick: Emerson yes (Wikimedia photo); Manitou, the lock and Dauphin no from photos; stone, log and frame buildings no; eight left '?'. Pictures: eight chosen with `photo_find.py` (three more have Wikipedia pictures), eight none.

## Tips
- The Manitoba Historical Society's "Abandoned Manitoba" page (`https://mhs.mb.ca/docs/sites/abandoned.shtml`) links about 400 site pages; grep its cached copy in `research/pages/` for `href="<slug>.shtml">` to get slugs and titles of ruins, kilns, quarries, dams and bridges in one go.
- MHS site coordinates were off the building for 6 of 19 (one in the middle of a street); `nom.py "<name>, <town>, Manitoba"` (or `osmnear.py <lat> <lon> 120 buildings` for Portage City Hall) gave OpenStreetMap points on the building each time.
- `photo_find.py set` wrote a pics file named with four letters of its own (`pics_sreg.py`), not the batch's letters; that is expected.
- parks.canada.ca visit pages carry old commented-out season notices beside the current one in `page.py --all` text (Riel House showed "see you in 2025" and "2027" together); read the dates carefully.
