## What this batch is

Twenty-nine Carnegie buildings in Virginia, West Virginia, Maryland and the District of Columbia, part of the pass the owner asked for to put the buildings Andrew Carnegie and the Rockefellers paid for on the map:
- Virginia: Norfolk's old main library and Van Wyck branch, Waynesboro (Fishburne Military School), William & Mary's Tucker Hall, the site of the Manassas Industrial School's Carnegie Building, Randolph-Macon's Peele Hall and Washington and Lee's Huntley Hall.
- West Virginia: Hinton, Huntington, Parkersburg and Bethany College's Cramblet Hall.
- Maryland: thirteen Baltimore branches of the Enoch Pratt Free Library (Brooklyn's site is gone).
- District of Columbia: the Mount Vernon Square library, Mount Pleasant, Southeast, Takoma Park and Howard University's Carnegie Building.

Delaware had no Carnegie library, by Wikipedia's state lists.

## Not checked

- **Baltimore:** Maryland's two inventory forms disagree on which branches the 1906 grant paid for: one names only branches 18 to 24, the other says fourteen including Easterwood. The batch follows Wikipedia's list of fourteen.
- **Baltimore, present use not found:** Keyworth, Locust Point (boarded and vacant at its nomination) and Mount Clare. Easterwood's church comes from an inventory form whose date was not checked.
- **Brooklyn (Baltimore):** whether the 1965 branch stands on the old library's ground; the pin marks the corner as area.
- **Pins marked site:** Waynesboro (which Fishburne building is the old library), Hinton (building not picked out on the aerial), Locust Point and Mount Clare (pins on the street crossings).
- **Bethany College:** which campus building is Cramblet Hall; the pin marks the campus.
- **Hours not found:** Hinton's veterans museum; Baltimore Clayworks (Mount Washington), not checked on its own site, so it is marked look-from-the-street.
- **Parkersburg and Norfolk main:** use today not found.
- **Manassas:** Wikipedia's list says an entrance arch survives; no other page confirmed it.
- **Brick:** red brick read only for Waynesboro (dark red brick, SAH Archipedia). Pale or stone walls from the sources for Huntington, Parkersburg, the Mount Vernon Square library and Mount Pleasant. The rest are not checked; Commons refused picture requests while the batch was researched.

## Tips for the next session

- **Baltimore:** the Pratt library's location history page (prattlibrary.org/about-us/history/location-history) gives opening and closing dates for every branch, and the Maryland Historical Trust form B-1387 (filed under that name, though it is B-1384 inside) gives branch numbers and architects.
- **Lookalike:** Randolph College in Lynchburg is not Randolph-Macon College in Ashland.
