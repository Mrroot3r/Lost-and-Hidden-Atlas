## What this batch is

Fifteen Carnegie buildings in eastern and central Pennsylvania, part of the pass the owner asked for to put the buildings Andrew Carnegie and the Rockefellers paid for on the map. Six are town libraries (Easton, Hamburg, Mansfield, Phoenixville, Reading and Ridley Park); nine are buildings Carnegie paid for at colleges and a medical society (Bucknell, the College of Physicians of Philadelphia, Grove City, Cheyney, Juniata, Lebanon Valley, Penn State, Perkiomen School and Swarthmore's Old Tarble). Philadelphia's branches and western Pennsylvania went in the two batches before this one; Johnstown's Cambria library (the Flood Museum) is in the western batch.

Sources: Wikipedia's list of Carnegie libraries in Pennsylvania (grants), each library's or college's own history pages, Wikipedia articles, SAH Archipedia, local papers, Joyce Tice's Tioga County pages (Mansfield's 1911 Gazette text), Swarthmore's engineering report on Old Tarble and the Swarthmore Phoenix, read with the web fetch tool. Hours are from the operators' own sites only. Pins were looked at on the leaf-off aerial.

Not checked:
- Swarthmore: which part of the building survived the 1983 arson fire. The college's Old Tarble page says the original Carnegie section survived and was renovated; its engineering report and a student history page say the fire destroyed the Carnegie section and only the 1928 addition stands. The place gives both.
- Mansfield: no source after 2001 says outright that the Carnegie building is still the library; the library's present address, 71 North Main Street, fits the 1911 corner of North Main and West Elmira Streets.
- Bucknell: what the Carnegie Building holds today differs between a 2003 and a 2016 source.
- Cheyney: no written source gave the hall's place on the campus; the pin is OpenStreetMap's Carnegie Hall on Alumni Drive, looked at on the aerial. Its walls and architect were not found.
- Red brick: "yes" for Hamburg, Grove City, Juniata and Perkiomen (Commons pictures looked at) and for the College of Physicians (its Wikipedia article: red brick with limestone trim); "no" where a source gives stone, limestone, ivory or yellow brick; "?" for Bucknell (brick, colour not stated), Cheyney and Penn State (nothing found; Penn State's only picture is a 1904 engraving).

Pictures: chosen from Wikimedia Commons by looking at the finder's sheet where one plainly shows the building; Penn State's is a 1904 engraving from the student paper. Bucknell, Cheyney, Lebanon Valley, Mansfield, Phoenixville, Reading and Swarthmore have none that fits (Swarthmore's "Tarble" picture on Commons shows the newer Tarble-in-Clothier, not Old Tarble).
